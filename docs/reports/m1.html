Gnomon · Milestone Report

M1 — Foundation

Naming, scope, scaffold. Shipped 2026-05-09.
TL;DR M1 ships no statistical machinery. It ships the conditions for one: a public, MIT-licensed repository under a name with three semantic layers, a product requirements document frozen at six features, and a Python scaffold that compiled, passed six tests, and went green on continuous integration on its first push — twenty-one seconds wall-clock. The deliverable is the boundary inside which M2 through M5 will be built. The temptation to begin M2 features during M1 was real and present throughout. It did not win.

Where Gnomon fits

The LLM evaluation ecosystem has names for two of its three layers. Discover is the verb that Anthropic's Petri operationalizes — automated auditing for concerning model behaviors. Quantify is the verb that Anthropic's Bloom and the benchmark-suite tradition operationalize — counting how often a behavior occurs. The third layer — Decide — is where teams currently make the most mistakes. They ship changes whose deltas fall inside the noise floor of the measurement. They run studies that were never powered to detect the effect of interest. They trust LLM-as-judge metrics that have not been calibrated against human raters. Gnomon is the statistical decision layer that completes the stack.

The naming decision

The internal codename was Gnomon from the strategic phase onward. During PRD drafting it was briefly renamed Aletheia — Greek ἀλήθεια, truth or disclosure — because the philosophical framing felt unusually clean for a project about epistemic care in evaluation. Pre-publish due diligence on the Aletheia name surfaced four collisions, listed below.

Collision · February 2026
DeepMind's Aletheia
Flagship Gemini Deep Think math research agent. Solved four open Erdős conjectures. Framed by DeepMind as "a homage to the Greek goddess of Truth" — the same etymological framing intended for Gnomon.
Collision · Trademark
Aletheia Framework™
Rolls-Royce ethical-AI governance framework, OECD-cataloged. Trademark held outside the eval space but adjacent enough to confuse SEO.
Collision · arXiv
Aletheia (2601.01532)
January 2026 paper on judge calibration in reasoning models. Uncomfortably adjacent to Gnomon's planned V1 judge-calibration feature.
Collision · GitHub
Youcef3939/aletheia
Existing XAI explainability framework. Smaller, but already indexed.

Four AI/ML projects with the same name, one of them a flagship Google effort, one of them on Gnomon's exact methodological territory. The naming brief was no longer "find a good name" but "find a name that the project can still own." Aletheia did not pass that bar. The decision reverted to the original codename. The full record lives in ADR-0001.

Three meanings of gnomon

A gnomon is the upright piece of a sundial that casts the shadow used to measure time. It is also a Euclidean geometric figure: the L-shaped piece that, when added to a smaller square, completes a larger one. The Greek root γνώμων means indicator or that which knows. Three semantic layers, all on-thesis for a statistical decision layer.

Indicator A statistical metric is an indicator. A ship/no-ship recommendation is an indicator. The project's job is to be the indicator — and to be honest about the precision of the reading.
Measurement through indirection A sundial does not measure the sun directly; it measures the shadow. LLM evaluation does not measure model behavior directly; it measures projections of it — outputs, traces, judge scores. Same epistemic shape. The artifact you have access to is always a degraded image of the artifact you care about.
The completing piece In Euclidean geometry the gnomon is what you add to a square to make it larger. In the three-layer stack — Discover, Quantify, Decide — Gnomon is the piece that completes the figure.

The naming triad

The three projects now form a coherent metaphor family: Petri (laboratory dish), Bloom (botanical growth), Gnomon (scientific instrument). Three different metaphor families, each tied to its layer's verb, all under the umbrella of scientific instruments.

Discover Petri
A dish. Where you grow things to look at them.
Quantify Bloom
Botanical growth. Counting what comes up.
Decide Gnomon
An instrument. Measures the shadow, not the sun.
Aletheia is a more poetic name. Gnomon is a stronger one. Letting go of the prettier name and keeping the more durable one is, recursively, the kind of decision Gnomon itself is designed to support. — ADR-0001

Six features, frozen

The PRD freezes V1 scope at six features. Anything not in this list does not get built before alpha (M5, due 2026-09-15). The V2 backlog catches the "wouldn't it be cool if" ideas — and there have already been several — but the contract is the PRD.

  1. Eval runner — Agent protocol, native runner, transcript ingestor (Bloom JSONL, Inspect logs, raw JSONL).
    V1
  2. Bootstrapped CIs on aggregate scores. Configurable resamples; percentile, basic, and BCa methods.
    V1
  3. Power analysis — sample-size recommendation given effect size, α, and target power.
    V1
  4. Judge calibration — Cohen's κ, Krippendorff's α, Spearman with bootstrapped CIs, bucketed agreement matrix.
    V1
  5. A/B harness — paired comparison with bootstrapped CIs on the difference; sequential testing with alpha-spending; pre-run power analysis.
    V1
  6. Storage + reports — SQLite + HTML + machine-readable JSON + markdown executive summary leading with Decision: SHIP / DON'T SHIP / UNDERPOWERED.
    V1
Out of scope for V1 — explicitly: distribution-shift detection, reward-model patterns, multi-judge ensembling, Bayesian A/B, multi-arm bandit, full Bloom adapter, Petri adapter, Streamlit dashboard, agent-trace eval primitives, sabotage/reward-hacking detection. If it is not in the list of six above, it does not get built before alpha. This is the most important sentence in the PRD.

Voice and rules

Every M1 commit carries the same constraints that every M2 commit will. They are recorded in CLAUDE.md and PRD.md so they survive future-context drift and personnel changes — including the maintainer's own context-window resets.

  • TL;DR upfront. Lead with the conclusion.
  • Honest limitations. Every report includes a "Gnomon is bad at: X, Y, Z" section. Non-negotiable.
  • Cite the lineage. Don't reinvent. Hashimoto et al. is named. Bloom and Petri are named. Inspect AI and HELM are named.
  • Precise language. "95% bootstrapped CI" — not "pretty confident."
  • Calm, curious, specific. Not promotional, not academic-stuffy.
  • Platform-neutral. No Microsoft-specific code paths. HTTPAgent is the universal escape hatch.
  • No customer data, ever. Synthetic datasets and public benchmarks only.
  • HTML-first reports. Per Thariq's "Unreasonable Effectiveness of HTML" (May 2026). Source files stay in markdown for git-diff sanity.

Honest limitations

M1 ships no statistical functionality. The list below is what is not in this milestone.

  • No confidence intervals. No power analyses. No judge calibrators. No eval results scored against anything.
  • The EvalRunner is a skeleton — it loops over cases and calls an agent, but no agent adapter exists yet for an actual model.
  • The Agent protocol has only an echo-style stub in tests. AnthropicAgent and OpenAIAgent are M2 and later.
  • The storage schema accepts a score column, but nothing populates it yet.
  • No HTML report generator. The report you are reading was hand-written; the templating that produces this kind of artifact from a BootstrapResult automatically is M4.

This is by design. Foundation milestones that try to ship features tend to ship unstable foundations.

A milestone you can mentally hold the whole boundary of is a milestone whose scope you tend to keep.
